Most surprising solar bills are correct: they are peak hours, settlement months and fixed charges at work (see why your bill is still high). But billing errors are real. NEPRA’s 2023 inquiry into DISCO overbilling found that not a single DISCO billed all its consumers correctly, with about Rs 8.44 billion overbilled in two months. Here is how to tell which kind of bill you have.

Errors that the bill itself can prove

ProblemHow to spot it
Misread meterPresent − previous ≠ units on any of the four registers
Exports missingExport registers show 0 or no change while your inverter shows export
Registers swappedImport and export figures look reversed compared with last month’s pattern
Estimated readingBill marked as estimated/average, or the reading doesn’t match your meter display
Long billing periodReading dates more than about 30 days apart
Net-metering not startedBill is still on the ordinary tariff after your green meter was fitted
Exports cappedExports credited lower than recorded, possibly due to the export-MDI check on capacity above your approval
Detection chargeA detection or adjustment line you don’t recognise

Solar Bill checks the first four automatically: it recomputes every register, compares the QR code with the print, and flags anything that doesn’t add up.

Collect evidence before you complain

  1. Meter photos. Net meters cycle through registers on the display. Photograph each one (import and export, peak and off-peak), with the date visible.
  2. Inverter data. Export the month’s generation and export figures from your inverter app, for the same dates as the bill.
  3. The bill PDF, downloaded from the PITC website (see how to download it).
  4. Your paperwork: net-metering agreement, commissioning date, and demand-notice receipt if the meter is not installed yet.

The government’s Power Smart app (“Apna Meter, Apni Reading”), launched in June 2025, lets you record your own monthly reading for DISCO consumers. It is useful evidence if readings are estimated.

Step 1: complain to your DISCO

  • Call 118 from any phone.
  • SMS 8118.
  • Email ccms@pitc.com.pk (PITC’s complaint management system, covering LESCO, IESCO, FESCO, GEPCO, MEPCO, PESCO, HESCO and others).
  • Visit your sub-division (SDO) office with copies of your evidence.

Always write down the complaint number. If you get no response, escalate with that number to qaccms@pitc.com.pk. Information about the PITC route is at bill.pitc.com.pk/ccms.

Step 2: complain to NEPRA

If the DISCO doesn’t resolve it, NEPRA’s Consumer Affairs Department handles complaints against distribution companies, and the 2026 Prosumer Regulations send prosumer–DISCO disputes to NEPRA as well.

  • File online: NEPRA complaint register.
  • Track it: NEPRA complaint tracking.
  • Include your 14-digit reference number, the bill, your evidence, and your DISCO complaint number.

NEPRA decisions are on record. In one October 2025 case, LESCO had never started net-metering billing after a meter change and then tried to charge 12,358 accumulated units at once; NEPRA limited the charge to 140 units a month, citing the Consumer Service Manual’s monthly reading rules.

Delayed green meters and stuck applications

In late 2025 and early 2026 some DISCOs stopped processing net-metering applications. NEPRA called this a violation, and on 26 August 2026 the Power Division told DISCOs and PITC to activate all cases where the demand notice was paid before 9 February 2026. Those cases are entitled to the old net-metering terms. If yours is stuck, complain with your demand-notice receipt and the payment date.

Subject: Incorrect net-metering bill, Ref No [14-digit reference], [Month Year]

My net-metering bill for [month] appears incorrect:
- [e.g. Export off-peak register shows 0 units; meter display shows
  present reading 30,240 against previous 29,322 (918 units).]
Attached: bill PDF, meter photos dated [date], inverter export report.
Please issue a corrected bill before the due date of [date].
